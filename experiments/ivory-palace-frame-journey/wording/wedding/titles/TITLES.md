# Sanskrit wedding title candidates — research list

Status: Owner approved WT-01, WT-06, WT-10 and WT-11 (Vivah Vidhi). The remaining titles are unapproved candidates. Fluent Sanskrit and family/officiant review still apply. WT-01 through WT-10 are Sanskrit lexical candidates; WT-11 preserves the reference’s Roman-letter invitation heading. Roman spelling uses IAST; a simpler client-facing transliteration may be prepared only after review. The list includes general wedding words and narrower ritual words; those narrower words must not be used as a whole-wedding title unless the family explicitly selects that rite.

| ID | Devanagari | IAST | Plain meaning | Use guidance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| WT-01 | विवाहः | Vivāhaḥ | Marriage/wedding | General, clear title; masculine nominative singular. |
| WT-02 | परिणयः | Pariṇayaḥ | Marriage; leading around | Literary wedding title; verify register. |
| WT-03 | उद्वाहः | Udvāhaḥ | Marriage/wedding | Classical synonym; may be unfamiliar to guests. |
| WT-04 | विवाहविधिः | Vivāhavidhiḥ | Marriage procedure/law | Closest Sanskrit form to source “Vivaah Vidhi”; ritual-focused, not a generic celebration label. |
| WT-05 | विवाहोत्सवः | Vivāhotsavaḥ | Wedding festivity/feast | Celebratory rather than ceremony-specific. |
| WT-06 | विवाहकर्म | Vivāhakarma | Wedding rites/action | Neuter dictionary headword; inflection and local usage require review. |
| WT-07 | पाणिग्रहणम् | Pāṇigrahaṇam | Taking the hand in marriage | Specific rite; only if it is actually performed and named by family. |
| WT-08 | पाणिग्रहः | Pāṇigrahaḥ | Taking the hand; marriage | Ritual-associated term; distinguish from WT-07. |
| WT-09 | सप्तपदी | Saptapadī | The seven steps | Specific rite, not a synonym for every Hindu wedding. |
| WT-10 | विवाहयज्ञः | Vivāhayajñaḥ | Marriage sacrifice/rite | Highly ritual-specific; not suitable unless officiant confirms. |
| WT-11 | विवाह विधि | Vivah Vidhi | Wedding rites (intended invitation heading) | Owner-approved wording from the reference; confirm spelling and ceremonial suitability locally. |

The Monier-Williams lexical entry glosses `vivāhavidhi` as **law of marriage**, so the source title “Vivaah Vidhi” should be treated as a modern invitation usage rather than asserted to have that exact classical ceremonial meaning. `vivāhotsava` is attested as “marriage feast”; `pāṇigrahaṇa` and `saptapadī` describe individual rites. The candidate list is for review, not ten interchangeable promises about the ceremony.
