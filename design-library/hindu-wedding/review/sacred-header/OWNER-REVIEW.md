## Cultural basis and open decisions

References and licence/provenance notes are in review-manifest.json. Sources comprise documented common Ganesha iconography, a public-domain Basohli catalogue record, Auckland Museum's CC BY 4.0 brass figure record (credited to Auckland Museum), and written Om-form documentation. No external reference pixels or copyrighted compositions were incorporated. Historical colour/material descriptions inform an original synthesis. Catalogue descriptions were available, but local museum/reference image downloads were unavailable; this limits the depth of visual source checking. These sources do not establish Mauritian family or temple suitability.

All Ganesha candidates intentionally use four arms, a goad, looped noose, raised palm and sweets bowl, and an anatomical-left/viewer-right trunk. The broken-tusk distinction is subtle in some candidates and especially at mobile size: please inspect head crops. The mouse/vahana was intentionally omitted; competent family/cultural review must confirm that this is suitable. No named tradition-specific form or universal Mauritian/Tamil compatibility is asserted. The botanical surrounds and peacock feathers are decorative additions within registered entries, not a claim that Ganesha rides a peacock.

The Om options depict the conventional Devanagari-style symbol. No Tamil Om alternative, mantra or readable sacred words were generated. Tamil families, mixed traditions, uncertain families and “Let Enveloped advise me” require human guidance; sacred artwork is excluded for “No sacred imagery”. HW-SACRED-003 interprets the registry's undefined Gold Sacred Motif as an ornamented Om relief, rather than introducing a new sacred symbol; its ivory lotus/pearl surround is disclosed as an actual generated difference. HW-SACRED-004 is described as a lotus medallion without an unsupported blessing claim.

Several prompts yielded richer filled/floral styles than their suggested outline medium (notably HW-GANESHA-004 and 008). Metadata and public descriptions identify the actual style. The Owner must approve those interpretations or request stricter line-art regenerations.
